# Guidelines, rules and FAQ

Paraphrased from the Guidelines tab (as of 23 Oct 2026). Re-check the live page before submitting.

## Judging criteria (each scored 1–5)

**1. Impact Potential & Innovation** — how much it would matter if it worked, and how new it is (for 4–5: genuinely new, not a replication of recent work).
1 negligible (no clear problem or novelty) · 2 limited (real problem, generic approach) · 3 moderate (clear problem, reasonable approach, some novelty) · 4 significant (important problem, original approach or neglected area; others could build on it) · 5 exceptional (critical problem, genuinely novel, clear theory of change).

**2. Execution Quality** — soundness of method, implementation and findings.
1 seriously flawed · 2 weak (missing validation, flawed design, incomplete) · 3 competent (solid for the time, interpretable, limitations acknowledged) · 4 strong (thorough, convincing validation, immediately useful) · 5 exceptional (ambitious and rigorous; surprising findings or unusually robust validation).

**3. Presentation & Clarity** — how clearly work, findings and impact are communicated.
1 incomprehensible · 2 hard to follow · 3 clear enough (problem, method, findings, limitations present) · 4 well presented · 5 exceptionally clear.

## Submission requirements

Required:
- Research report PDF using the official template: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Y5wd3VRbXU5N08BvAGiJUQTaWbMCSPjhzXsR-ozuehg/copy?usp=sharing
- **Maximum 8 pages**, not counting references and appendices.
- Title and **abstract of 150–250 words**.
- Author names and affiliations.
- **"Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations" appendix.**

Optional: public GitHub repo; 3–5 minute video demo.

Submission form fields: title, summary, PDF (≤ 25 MB), interest in publishing (yes/no), tracks (tick all that fit), presentation recording, project code, slides (≤ 25 MB), project image, additional material, team name, location, team members (name, email, Discord, Google Scholar). Submitting means agreeing to the prize terms (https://apartresearch.com/sprints/prize-terms).

## Recommended report structure (most strong projects 4–8 pages)

- Introduction: the question and why it matters.
- Related Work: what you build on.
- Methodology: enough to replicate (models, prompts, environments, seeds, metrics).
- Results: quantitative where possible; report variance, seeds and baselines; **always include the positive control**.
- Discussion: implications, limitations, future work.
- Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations (required): what the result does and does not establish; any way the setup or findings could make real-world collusion easier or harder to detect.
- References.

## AI tools

Allowed in any way that helps (reasoning checks, drafting, code, writing). The team is responsible
for everything in the report; made-up results or references can disqualify a project. A judge can
decline to score a report too unclear to follow.

## Important notes

- Solo or team; up to 5 recommended, larger allowed.
- Each person can be on **at most two projects**.
- Building on existing work is allowed and encouraged, but clearly identify what was new during the sprint. Undisclosed prior work can lead to disqualification. **Mark code written before the sprint as preexisting in the README and the report.**
- Fixing or resubmitting: submit again before the deadline with the exact same title and details; new files replace old ones.
- Submit through the official form on the sprint page.
- Support: Discord help-desk (tag @Support) or sprints@apartresearch.com.
- Pre-submission checklist: template used; ≤ 8 pages before references and appendices; abstract 150–250 words; authors and affiliations; Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical appendix; links work.

## FAQ (summary)

**Getting started**
- How it works: sign up, join Discord, form/join a team or go solo, pick a track and question, build, submit a PDF report by the deadline; talks and Q&A throughout.
- Length: three days, 23–25 Oct; due Sunday 11:59 PM AoE.
- Remote: yes, everything online via Discord and Zoom; hubs optional.
- NYC hub: sign up and select New York City; details shared with registrants. Hong Kong hub: sign up, then register on the AI Safety Hong Kong hub page.
- Teams form before or during; team-forming channels on Discord.
- Tracks don't affect scoring; same rubric for all.
- No specific background required.
- No GPU or budget required; every practical start runs on a 16 GB laptop with low-cost APIs.
- Talks are recorded; recordings on Discord and the Schedule tab.
- Not required to attend all three days; only hard deadline is Sunday.
- Open globally.

**Submissions**
- Submit a research report (mini paper), not a product demo; include the required appendix.
- Always use the template linked on the Guidelines tab (acceptance-email versions may be older).
- Confirmation arrives shortly after submitting; if not, email.
- Published projects appear manually, up to 12 hours later.
- Fix mistakes by resubmitting with the exact same title.
- Team members can be added after submitting via the form.
- Unfinished work can and should be submitted; honest limitations welcome.
- AI tools allowed; team responsible; fabrication can disqualify.
- Building on existing research allowed if new work is clearly identified; mark preexisting code.
- Up to two projects per person, each with a unique title.
- No required citation style; be consistent.

**Judging and results**
- Expert judges review the PDF using the rubric, typically within about a week after the event.
- Individual scores stay internal; constructive feedback is shared without reviewer names.
- Results typically 1–2 weeks after the judging deadline; winners contacted directly; all participants get reviewer feedback by email.
- Fellowship fast-track means accelerated consideration, not a guaranteed place.
