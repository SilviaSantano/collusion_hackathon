# Overview

## What it is

A three-day research sprint (hackathon) on collusion between AI agents: when it emerges in markets
and everyday agent deployments, how an auditor can tell it apart from coincidence, how the
coordination is carried, and how a defender can break it. Participants pick one open question,
build a controlled experiment that answers it, and write it up. No AI-safety background required.

The page's motivation: earlier experiments have already produced AI collusion (pricing above
competitive levels, independent anti-competitive behaviour, and more than half of tested models
showing a coalition advantage when allowed to talk privately in a workplace task). It also points to
the OpenAI / Hugging Face incident as a recent example of agent communication and collusion
(link on the page: https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/openai-huggingface).

The sprint asks three questions:
1. When does collusion emerge, and which levers cause it?
2. What should count as convincing evidence of collusion, using logged agent trajectories?
3. How is the needed coordination carried, and how can it be broken (ideally before, otherwise during)?

## Format

- Teams of 1–5 pick a track, come up with a project, and ship. No prior participation, team or background required.
- Every research question is tied to existing papers and open-source code; the Resources tab gives starter code, a weekend-sized project and a rough cost per question.
- All default routes run on a normal 16 GB laptop without a GPU.

## Who should join

Engineers, researchers and people from any industry; mixed teams tend to do best. No AI/ML/safety
background required. Quantitative finance professionals are especially welcome (some tracks are
written so market intuition and data skills transfer).
